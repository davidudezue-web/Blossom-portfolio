Sales Performance Analysis & Executive Dashboard

A transaction-level sales analysis designed to make revenue performance easier to monitor across products, customer groups, and branch locations.

Project objective

The objective was to organise sales records into an executive-friendly report that supports monitoring of revenue, quantity sold, product contribution, customer mix, and branch performance. The dashboard includes interactive selections so users can explore the data by customer type, transaction, date, and branch.

Business questions

  • Which products contribute the most revenue?
  • How is recorded revenue distributed across branches?
  • Which customer segments account for the largest share of sales?
  • What summary indicators should managers monitor?

Scope and limitations

  • 30 recorded transactions covering 3–17 January 2025.
  • Analysis is descriptive and limited to the supplied records.
  • Revenue is not the same as profit; cost and margin data were not provided in this workbook.
  • The period is short and should not be treated as a long-term trend.

Data preparation and validation

01 · StructureReviewed the structured transaction table and its fields: date, product, quantity, unit price, branch, customer type, and total.
02 · Quality checksChecked the 30 transaction totals against quantity × unit price. All supplied records passed this arithmetic check.
03 · ReconciliationCompared the executive-summary narrative with the underlying records and identified mismatched product and customer-segment claims for correction.

The case study describes the validation work performed on the supplied workbook. Additional Power Query, DAX, or data-modelling techniques should only be claimed where they can be confirmed against the original Power BI file.

Verified findings from the supplied workbook

Branch comparison: Masaka accounts for the larger share of recorded revenue in the reviewed period.
Product mix: Dangote Cement is the highest-revenue product in the supplied transaction records.
Customer mix: Contractors are the highest-revenue customer segment in the supplied records.
Data quality: The transaction totals reconcile arithmetically, but some statements in the workbook's original written summary do not match the underlying data.

Commercial figures not necessary to explain the findings have been omitted from this public case study.

Recommended next steps

  • Ensure the published screenshots and transaction-derived findings remain within the scope of the company's permission.
  • Correct the written executive summary so all percentages and product totals reconcile with the transaction table.
  • Review the average transaction value measure and confirm whether it is calculated per transaction or per other unit.
  • Consider adding date-range filtering, clearer number formatting, and more restrained visual styling.
  • Keep the final case study and supporting screenshots available through the portfolio website.
