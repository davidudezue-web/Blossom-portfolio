Sales Performance Analysis & Executive Dashboard

A transaction-level sales analysis designed to help decision-makers monitor recorded revenue, evaluate sales performance, and explore product, customer, and branch-level results.

Project Objective

The objective was to transform transaction records into an executive-friendly Power BI report. The analysis focuses on revenue, quantity sold, product contribution, customer mix, and branch performance. Interactive filters allow users to investigate the available records from different perspectives.

Business Questions

  • Which products generate the highest recorded revenue?
  • How is revenue distributed across branch locations?
  • Which customer segments contribute the most revenue?
  • What key performance indicators should management monitor?
  • Do transaction totals reconcile with the underlying records?

Scope and Limitations

  • 30 recorded transactions covering 3–17 January 2025.
  • The analysis describes the supplied records rather than predicting future performance.
  • Revenue is not profit. Cost, operating expenses, and margin data were not provided.
  • The short observation period does not establish long-term trends or seasonality.
  • Findings depend on the completeness and accuracy of the supplied source data.

Data Preparation and Validation

01 · Data Structure Reviewed the transaction table and its available fields, including transaction date, product, quantity, unit price, branch, customer type, and recorded total.
02 · Arithmetic Validation Compared the supplied transaction totals against the expected quantity multiplied by unit price. The reported validation result indicates that the 30 supplied records passed this arithmetic check.
03 · Reconciliation Compared the written executive summary with the underlying transaction records to identify statements that required correction.

The validation process distinguishes between arithmetic consistency and overall data quality. A transaction can reconcile mathematically while the dataset still contains missing information, classification issues, or misleading summary statements.

Power Query transformations, DAX measures, and data-model configurations should be described in detail only where they can be confirmed against the original Power BI project file.

Key Business Findings

Revenue measurement: The reviewed records contain total recorded revenue of ₦3,339,100 across 30 transactions.
Average transaction value: Total recorded revenue divided by the number of transactions produces an average transaction value of approximately ₦111,303.33.
Product performance: The product-level analysis supports comparisons of recorded revenue across the products represented in the dataset. Product rankings should be interpreted alongside transaction volume and unit pricing.
Branch and customer analysis: Branch and customer-type breakdowns help identify differences in recorded sales contribution. Specific rankings should be confirmed against the source records before publication.
Data quality: Arithmetic reconciliation is an important validation step, but written summaries must also agree with the underlying transaction-level results.

These findings are descriptive. The dataset does not, by itself, establish the causes of revenue differences or demonstrate profitability.

Business Recommendations

  • Improve KPI consistency: Standardise revenue and transaction-value calculations so the dashboard and written reports use the same definitions.
  • Monitor product contribution: Review product-level revenue alongside quantities sold and, when available, product costs and gross margins.
  • Investigate branch differences: Compare revenue and transaction volumes across locations before drawing conclusions about branch performance.
  • Strengthen reporting controls: Reconcile summary statements with the underlying records before management reports are distributed.
  • Expand the observation period: Include additional months of reliable data before making claims about growth, seasonality, or long-term performance.
