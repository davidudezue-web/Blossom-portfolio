Project objective

```

The objective was to organise sales records into an executive-friendly report that supports monitoring of recorded sales value, quantity sold, product contribution, customer mix, and branch performance. The dashboard includes interactive selections so users can explore the data by customer type, transaction, date, and branch.

Business questions

  • Which products contribute the most recorded sales value?
  • How is recorded sales value distributed across branches?
  • Which customer segments account for the largest share of sales?
  • What summary indicators should managers monitor?

Scope and limitations

  • 30 recorded transactions covering 3–17 January 2025.
  • Analysis is descriptive and limited to the supplied records.
  • Sales value is not the same as profit; cost and margin data were not provided in this workbook.
  • The period is short and should not be treated as a long-term trend.
```

Data preparation and validation

```
01 · Structure Reviewed the structured transaction table and its fields: date, product, quantity, unit price, branch, customer type, and total.
02 · Quality checks Checked the 30 transaction totals against quantity × unit price. All supplied records passed this arithmetic check.
03 · Reconciliation Compared the executive-summary narrative with the underlying records and identified mismatched product and customer-segment claims for correction.

This case study describes the validation performed on the supplied workbook. Additional Power Query, DAX, or data-modelling techniques should only be claimed where they can be confirmed against the original Power BI file.

Key Findings & Business Insights

```

The findings below describe the supplied transaction records for 3–17 January 2025. Category percentages are calculated against the reported total sales value of ₦3,339,100 and should be reconciled against the original workbook before publication.

01 · Branch performance

Masaka recorded sales value of ₦1,980,700, approximately 59.32% of the reported total. The other branch accounted for ₦1,358,400, or approximately 40.68%.

Business implication: Compare transaction counts, product mix, units sold, and average transaction value between branches to investigate the difference.

02 · Product contribution

Dangote Cement generated a reported sales value of ₦887,500, approximately 26.58% of total recorded sales value.

Business implication: Review sales volume, stock availability, pricing, and purchasing patterns before making inventory or promotional decisions.

03 · Customer segment contribution

Contractors generated a reported sales value of ₦1,352,400, approximately 40.50% of total recorded sales value.

Business implication: Examine repeat purchases, order frequency, and product preferences to identify opportunities to strengthen customer relationships.

04 · Average transaction value

Using the reported total of ₦3,339,100 across 30 transactions, the average transaction value is approximately ₦111,303.33.

Business implication: Monitor average transaction value alongside transaction count and units sold to understand changes in recorded sales value.

05 · Data validation

The supplied transaction totals were checked against quantity multiplied by unit price. Arithmetic consistency is useful, but it does not independently establish data completeness or the accuracy of the underlying business records.

Business implication: Retain validation checks and reconcile dashboard totals with the source workbook before decisions are made.

Interpretation note: The analysis covers a short observation period. It does not establish long-term trends, profitability, causation, or future performance. Validate the category figures against the original transaction data before relying on them for business decisions.
```

Management Recommendations

```

These are proposed follow-up actions based on the observed results, not claims that the actions have already been implemented.

01 · Review branch performance

Compare transaction counts, units sold, product mix, and average transaction value between Masaka and the other branch.

Purpose: Investigate the factors associated with the difference in recorded sales value between the branches.

02 · Monitor product contribution

Review sales volume, inventory availability, pricing, and purchasing patterns for Dangote Cement and the other products.

Purpose: Support evidence-based inventory and product-management decisions.

03 · Understand customer segments

Compare transaction frequency, order size, product preferences, and repeat-purchase behaviour across the three customer segments.

Purpose: Identify opportunities to improve customer relationships and develop more targeted sales strategies.

04 · Expand the reporting period

Add more historical transactions and report on a consistent weekly or monthly basis.

Purpose: Enable more reliable trend analysis, period comparisons, and monitoring of changes in business performance.

Financial reporting limitation: Before evaluating profitability, incorporate validated cost data and define the appropriate profit and margin measures. Recorded sales value alone does not establish profitability.
